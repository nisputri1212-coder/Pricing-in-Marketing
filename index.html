Pricing Strategy Assessment

Profit and Sales

Setting pricing objectives based on desired profit margins or maximizing sales volume. For example, marketers might sacrifice sales on one item to encourage customers to purchase a different product that carries a higher profit margin (like a store brand).

Market Share

Pricing products to capture a specific segment of the market or a larger percentage of total industry sales. When competition gets intense and the market shrinks, firms often resort to price reductions or bundle pricing to maintain their market share.

Competitive Effect (Market-Based Pricing)

Pricing a product based on (above, below, or the same as) the competition's pricing. Marketers deliberately adjust prices to dilute a competitor's marketing efforts, preempt their pricing changes, or safely maintain profitability in an oligopoly (like airlines).

Image Enhancement

Using higher prices to communicate prestige, high quality, or a cutting-edge reputation. For "prestige products" (like luxury cars or designer bags), a higher price actually increases consumer desirability among status-conscious buyers.

Costs and Demand

Understanding the internal math of the product. This includes estimating consumer demand at different price points (price elasticity) and combining variable costs (per-unit costs that fluctuate) with fixed costs to determine the minimum break-even price.

Environmental Factors

Adjusting prices based on broad external influences. This includes adapting to economic trends (inflation, recessions/business cycle), responding to government regulations, adapting to the international environment, and shifting consumer trends.
